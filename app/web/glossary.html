Glossary

What each term on the page means, and how the page calculates it. Every number comes from the match data; the full method is in docs/METRICS.md in the repository.

Win chance

Win chance
The chance that the home club wins, that the match is a draw, and that the away club wins, at that moment. The three add up to 100%. A scoring-rate model: Elo ratings before kick-off, then the score, the time left, the expected goals so far and red cards. Tested on 1,520 real Premier League matches it never saw.
Elo rating
A strength rating for each club, calculated only from its earlier real results. A win against a strong club adds more than a win against a weak one.
Brier score
How good a forecast is: the mean squared error of the chances against what happened. Lower is better. At half-time the model scores 0.473; a table of half-time scores scores 0.503.
Box score copy
Each synthetic match copies the totals of one real Premier League match: goals in each half, shots, shots on target, corners, fouls and cards. Clubs, players and play are fictional.

Shots

xG (expected goals)
The chance that a shot from that position becomes a goal, from 0 to 1. A team's xG is the sum of its shots' xG. From the distance to goal and the angle the shooter can see. About 0.17 from 11 m straight on, 0.09 from 16 m, 0.03 from 25 m. A header counts half. A penalty is 0.76.
Big chance, half chance, long shot
Labels for a shot's xG: a big chance is 0.30 or more, a half chance 0.10 to 0.30, a long shot under 0.10. The verifier checks that a label fits the number.
On target
A shot that would go in without a save: a goal or a save.

Team stats, last 10 minutes

Field tilt
Who has the territory. A team's passes in its attacking third, divided by both teams' passes in their attacking thirds. 50% is even; 70% means the team is camped in the other half.
PPDA (passes per defensive action)
How hard a team presses. The opponent's passes in its own 60% of the pitch, divided by this team's tackles won, interceptions and fouls in that area. Lower means a harder press. Real teams: about 8 to 15. Capped at 30.
Duels won
The share of duels a team wins. A duel is two players contesting the ball: on the ground (a 50-50, a tackle or a take-on) or in the air (a header from a long ball, a cross, a corner, a goal kick or a clearance). The winner keeps the ball.
50-50
A loose ball that both teams have the same chance to win. Counted as a ground duel.
Possession share
The share of the last 5 minutes that a team had the ball.
Momentum
Who is on top right now, from 0 to 1. Recent shots (by xG), passes and runs into the box, recoveries in the attacking third and set pieces count; older actions count less. Labels: on top (0.65 or more), even, under pressure (under 0.35). It is a rule of thumb, not a fitted model.
Pressing intensity
A team's pressure events divided by the opponent's passes, over the last 5 minutes. Labels: intense press (0.6 or more), normal press (0.3 to 0.6), sitting off (under 0.3).
Line height and block
How far up the pitch a team defends: the average distance of its four deepest outfield players from its own goal line. A high block is 40 m or more, a mid block 25 to 40 m, a low block under 25 m.

Play

Pressure, under pressure
An opponent closes down the player on the ball. A pass made under pressure is marked, and it is completed less often.
Through ball
A pass into the space behind the defence for a runner. The page shows the runner's position at the moment of the pass.
Offside line, offside margin
The line of the second-last defender (the goalkeeper usually counts as one). A player beyond it, in the opponent's half, at the moment of the pass is offside. The margin is how far beyond (or behind) the line he was, in metres.
Clearance
A defender sends the ball away from his goal: upfield, out for a throw-in or behind for a corner.
Ball height
How high the ball is, in metres. A lofted ball flies in an arc; the page draws its shadow on the grass and writes the height when it is over 1 m.

Dead ball

Dead ball, restart
The ball is out of play: a throw-in, goal kick, corner, free kick, penalty or kick-off follows. Each restart waits as long as the Premier League average for its kind (a corner about 36 seconds).
Set piece
A restart that can lead straight to a chance: a corner, a free kick in shooting range or a penalty.
What if
On the dead-ball card: how often this kind of set piece becomes a goal (about 3 in 100 corners, 6 in 100 direct free kicks, 78 in 100 penalties), and the team's win chance now and if it goes in.
Finding
On the dead-ball card: the biggest change in the last 5 minutes compared with the match before them (duels, passing, territory or shots), and whether it is unusual.
p-value, unusual
The chance of a change at least this large if nothing had really changed. Eight things are checked at every pause, so the smallest p-value is multiplied by 8 (the Bonferroni correction). The card says "unusual" only when that corrected value is below 0.05; otherwise it says normal variation.

Running

Speed zones
The zones FIFA uses in its World Cup physical reports: walking 0 to 7 km/h, jogging 7 to 15, running 15 to 20, high-speed running 20 to 25, sprinting over 25.
Sprint
A run of one or more seconds over 25 km/h. It counts once, however long it lasts.
Top speed
A player's fastest second, in km/h.
Pace per 90
A player's distance scaled to 90 minutes, compared with the Premier League mean for his position. Within 1 standard deviation is typical.

Pitch views

Trails
The last 4 seconds of each player's path, colored by speed: pale is running, solid is high-speed running, pink is a sprint. Walking and jogging leave no trail.
Heat map
Where a team, a player or an official covered ground, in 18 zones. Each zone shows its share of the distance.
Shape (formation)
The formation a team played in each 15-minute block, with and without the ball: the template closest to the players' average positions, and how far they were from it on average.
Player camera
A 3D view from a player's eyes during a key moment. Players are drawn as simple capsules in their kit colors. Hold the middle mouse button and move the mouse to turn his head.

Fact checks

Narrator agent
A Microsoft Foundry model, run with Microsoft Agent Framework, that writes each comment. It calls tools for the facts and cites each fact with its source.
Verifier
Code that checks every claim before it airs: each number against its stored value, each label against its number, and that no source comes from later in the match. It also stops numbers with no source and "because" with no measured change.
Passed first time, corrected on retry, template
A comment that fails goes back to the agent once with the list of failures. If it passes then, it is "corrected on retry". If it fails again, a plain template sentence airs instead.
Viewers (personas)
The same match, three ways: an analyst (numbers allowed), a home fan, and a fan who follows one player. Each gets different moments and different words, in English or Spanish.
